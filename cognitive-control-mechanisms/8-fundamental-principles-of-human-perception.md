## 1. Conscious Mind
What we are aware of at this moment: active thoughts and conscious decision-making. Accounts for approximately 5–10% of total processing.
Associated Brain Regions:
* Prefrontal Cortex (PFC), especially Dorsolateral PFC (DLPFC): Logical thinking, planning, and problem-solving.
* Ventromedial / Orbitofrontal PFC: Value assessment and decision-making.
* Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC): Conflict detection and attention control.
* Parietal Cortex: Spatial self-awareness and sensory information integration.
What we call "thinking right now" is usually the result of the Frontoparietal Network working together, which is the primary system for self-awareness and intentional control.

## 2. Preconscious / Subconscious
Information not currently in thought but immediately recallable, such as names, memories, and skills practiced to automaticity. Acts as a buffer layer between conscious and unconscious.
Associated Brain Regions:
* Hippocampus: Episodic memory retrieval.
* Medial Temporal Lobe: Storage and retrieval of learned information.
* Prefrontal Cortex: Controls memory retrieval.
* Default Mode Network (DMN): Network active when the brain is not focused on external tasks, such as thinking about oneself, the past, or the future.
This layer acts like a buffer between what is in conscious awareness and the vast amount of data stored in the brain.

## 3. Unconscious Mind
Stores repressed material, trauma, and conflicting desires. Difficult to access directly, yet constantly drives behavior.
Associated Brain Regions (Approximate):
* Amygdala: Fear, threat evaluation, and emotional learning.
* Hypothalamus: Basic drives such as hunger, sexual desire, and survival.
* Basal Ganglia: Automatic behavior patterns and habits.
* Brainstem: Fundamental systems of arousal and instinctive, unthought responses.
Freud did not claim any specific brain region directly corresponds to the unconscious, but from a neuroscientific perspective, what "drives behavior prior to awareness" usually involves the limbic system and subcortical automatic circuits.

## 4. Personal Unconscious
An individual's forgotten or repressed experiences, one's shadow, and unaccepted parts of oneself that nevertheless remain present.
Associated Brain Regions (Approximate):
* Hippocampus: Personal memories.
* Amygdala: Emotionally intense memories, particularly shame, fear, and rejection.
* Medial Prefrontal Cortex (mPFC): Evaluation of self and self-image.
* Posterior Cingulate Cortex (PCC): Linking memories with identity.
The concept of the "shadow" can be connected to neural circuits related to self-representation and emotionally tagged memory, rather than having a single specific brain location.

## 5. Collective Unconscious
A layer deeper than the individual; a shared structure among all human beings. Archetypes reside here—Hero, Shadow, Anima, Animus, Self—inherited through evolution rather than personal experience.
Associated Brain Regions (Functional Hypothesis):
* Default Mode Network (DMN): Construction of narratives about self and others.
* Temporoparietal Junction (TPJ): Understanding the intentions of others (theory of mind).
* Temporal Lobes: Processing symbols, language, and narratives.
* Evolutionarily conserved circuits: Shared evolutionary circuits, such as face recognition, maternal instinct, cooperation, and threat detection.
Currently, there is no evidence that a distinct "collective unconscious layer" exists in the brain, but there is evidence that humans possess shared evolutionary circuits that lead to similar responses to certain symbols, roles, and narratives worldwide.

## 6. Somatic / Body Memory
The body stores trauma and experience without relying on language. Bessel van der Kolk — "The Body Keeps the Score". Muscles, the autonomic nervous system, and responses occurring prior to thought.
Associated Brain & Neural Systems:
* Insular Cortex (Insula): Perception of internal bodily states (interoception).
* Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC): Both physical and emotional pain.
* Amygdala: Linking experiences to threat perception.
* Periaqueductal Gray (PAG): Freeze response and self-defense.
* Vagus Nerve + Autonomic Nervous System: Regulation of heart rate, respiration, and the digestive system.
"Body memory" does not mean muscle tissue stores memory like a hard drive; rather, it means neural circuits connecting the brain and body have been rewired such that the body automatically responds when encountering similar stimuli.

## 7. Implicit Memory
Skills, habits, and automatic responses that do not require conscious thought. Driving, walking, or speaking one's native language—embedded within procedural memory managed by the cerebellum and basal ganglia.
Associated Brain Regions:
* Basal Ganglia (specifically Putamen): Habit formation and movement sequences.
* Cerebellum: Precision, timing, and motor learning.
* Supplementary Motor Area (SMA): Preparing and executing movement sequences.
* Motor Cortex: Motor command execution.
This is one of the most clearly evidenced systems in neuroscience. As a skill is repeatedly practiced, neural circuits shift from PFC control over to the basal ganglia + cerebellum, allowing execution without extensive conscious thought.

## 8. Autonomic Processing
Autonomic nervous system—Sympathetic and Parasympathetic—operates prior to conscious awareness. Fight/flight/freeze/fawn responses trigger here before language can even form.
Associated Brain Regions:
* Hypothalamus: Control center for body balance (homeostasis).
* Amygdala: Rapid threat detection.
* Brainstem (Medulla, Pons): Regulation of breathing, heart rate, and blood pressure.
* Sympathetic Nervous System: Accelerated response (fight / flight).
* Parasympathetic Nervous System (especially Vagus nerve): Recovery, rest, and digestion.
* Periaqueductal Gray (PAG): Freeze / defensive immobility.
This system can respond within tens to hundreds of milliseconds—long before the prefrontal cortex can construct a linguistic explanation.

## Hierarchical Overview

| Layer | Primary Brain Regions |
| --- | --- |
| Conscious | Prefrontal Cortex, ACC, Parietal |
| Preconscious | Hippocampus, Medial Temporal, DMN |
| Unconscious | Amygdala, Hypothalamus, Basal Ganglia |
| Personal Unconscious | Hippocampus, Amygdala, mPFC |
| Collective Unconscious | DMN, TPJ, Temporal circuits (Hypothetical) |
| Somatic Memory | Insula, ACC, PAG, Vagus/ANS |
| Implicit Memory | Basal Ganglia, Cerebellum, Motor circuits |
| Autonomic Processing | Hypothalamus, Brainstem, Amygdala, ANS |

An interesting observation is that ordering from Conscious → Autonomic reveals a cascade from the Prefrontal Cortex (cortex; thinking and language) down to the Limbic System (emotion and drives), and deepest to the Brainstem & Autonomic Nervous System (fundamental survival). This aligns with the concept that the deeper the layer, the faster, more automatic, and harder it is to intentionally control.

Consciousness is merely the tip of the iceberg; the remaining 90%+ operates below without conscious awareness. Intriguingly, most decisions are initiated in lower layers before consciousness realizes and assumes it made the choice.
