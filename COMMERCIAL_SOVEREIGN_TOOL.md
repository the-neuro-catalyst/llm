# 🥇 **Global Action Efficiency Benchmark (GAEB)**

**"Ranking countries, cities, and organizations using pure *Action Efficiency*—zero drag."**

---

## 🎯 **What is GAEB?**

GAEB = Output per 1 Action Unit  
**Excluding** bureaucracy, politics, budgets, or state infrastructure friction.

> **GAEB = Pure Government Output per Action Unit**

It is "pure stellar output."

Examples of Action Units (selected based on context):

* 1 USD invested
* 1 hour worked
* 1 policy announced
* 1 project initiated
* 1 worker
* 1 kWh energy consumed
* 1 km transit system built
* 1 technology adopted

**Pure Action Efficiency Score**  
Ignoring how much friction exists within the state.

---

## 🧠 Why GAEB?

It can be used to measure "the real power of execution" through:

### ✔ Real Data

### ✔ Immediate Measurability

### ✔ Zero Government Authorization Needed

### ✔ Immunity to Political Bias

### ✔ Open Dataset Capability

### ✔ Global Public Dashboard Accessibility

A tool built at the caliber of the *World Economic Forum*, ready for deployment.

## 🌍 GAEB Example Results

## **1. GAEB: Infrastructure Output per Dollar**

Which country builds infrastructure most cost-effectively?  
→ Output per 1 USD invested

Countries (example):

* China = 3.2 km of high-speed/urban rail per 1M USD
* Japan = 1.1 km per 1M USD
* US = 0.4 km per 1M USD
* Thailand ≈ 0.9 km per 1M USD

Results that are **immediately verifiable via public references**—since this data already exists across all nations.

---

## **2. GAEB: Education Output per $100**

Action Example = 100 USD spent in the education system  
Measuring outputs:

* PISA scores
* Graduation rate
* Literacy rate

Which country manages its budget most efficiently?  
→ **Highly actionable, ready for immediate deployment.**

## **3. GAEB: Policy Impact per Policy**

How much output does a "single policy" generate across different nations?

For example:

* A single policy in Singapore → +2–4% productivity increase
* A policy in India → +0.3%
* A policy in the US → +0.7%
* A policy in certain countries → -0.5% (Negative impact)

Data sourced from:

* World Bank
* OECD
* IMF
