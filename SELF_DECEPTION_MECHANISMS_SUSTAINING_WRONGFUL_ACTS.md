## **INTENT**

**Intent** lurks and operates across **every stage of the process**, though it changes form and fulfills different roles at each step. The true starting point of intent primarily occurs in the **"Pre-behavior Stage"**:

#### **1. Pre-behavior Stage: Intent as the "Commander and Employer"**
This is where intent operates most intensely, occurring prior to self-rationalization.
* **Intent precedes rationale:** The desire for gain or the avoidance of hardship drives the **"intent to commit a wrong"** into existence first.
* **Intent hires the brain to find excuses:** Once intent locks onto its target, the analytical brain acts like a **"lawyer,"** sourcing rationales to unlock guilt so that intent can proceed unhindered.

#### **2. During-behavior Stage: Intent as the "Controller"**
At this stage, intent manifests into an overt act.
* **Controlling for execution:** Intent oversees the progress of the wrongdoing to ensure successful execution according to plan.
* **Using rationale as a shield:** While acting, intent uses the rationales manufactured in the first stage as a psychological shield to reduce panic and maintain composure until the mission is complete.

#### **3. Post-behavior Stage: Intent as the "Protector and Survivor"**
Once the act is completed, intent shifts its objective from **"desiring gain"** to **"surviving detection and stigma."**
* **Self-protective intent:** This drives the brain to generate post-hoc rationalizations or distort memories to completely deny responsibility.
* **Becoming a permanent intent:** If successful and unpunished, committing the next offense becomes faster and easier to initiate in the future.

## **RATIONALIZATION**

The working mechanism of **"rationalization"** to evade wrongdoing or reduce guilt during dishonest acts is a psychological process designed to protect one's Ego.

### **Core Mechanisms and Processes**
This process is driven by three main psychological patterns aimed at turning a **"wrongful act"** into something **"acceptable"** within one's mind:

#### **1. Cognitive Dissonance**
* Humans dislike the conflicting feeling between "viewing themselves as a good person" and the "bad behavior they are about to commit."
* The brain must construct new rationales to restore balance, allowing the individual to commit bad acts while still feeling like a good person.

#### **2. Techniques of Neutralization**
* **Denial of Responsibility:** "I had to do it; circumstances forced me," or "Everybody does it."
* **Denial of Injury:** "The company is filthy rich; this tiny amount won't hurt them."
* **Denial of the Victim:** "They asked to be cheated," or "They got what they deserved."
* **Condemnation of the Condemners:** "The people criticizing me have done wrong things too."
* **Appeal to Higher Loyalties:** "I only cheated this money to pay for my parents' medical bills."

#### **3. Cost-Benefit Rationalization**
* The brain weighs the anticipated benefits against the likelihood of getting caught.
* If the benefits are high, the brain immediately works overtime to dig up rationales to support the offense.

## **Timeline of the Process**

This process unfolds across three primary timeframes, spanning from before the act to after its completion:

#### **1. Pre-behavior Stage: Unlocking the Heart's Door**
* **Hardship or desire arises:** Facing a financial shortage or seeking additional gain.
* **Dishonest thoughts emerge:** The brain spots an opportunity for wrongdoing.
* **Immediate internal conflict:** Conscience warns that "it is wrong."
* **Anticipatory Rationalization:** The brain begins manufacturing supporting arguments, such as "Just this once," or "I'll return it next month once things settle." Once the logic aligns, the door unlocks for action.

#### **2. During-behavior Stage: Deflecting Attention**
* **Executing the wrong:** The brain temporarily blocks feelings of guilt.
* **Focusing on the target:** Concentration centers entirely on completing the task and evading capture.
* **Reinforcing rationales:** During execution, an inner voice may continually reiterate the initial excuses to maintain composure and prevent panic.

#### **3. Post-behavior Stage: Constructing a Permanent Shield**
* **Paranoia/Guilt emerges (after things settle):** Fear of getting caught or feelings of guilt begin to attack.
* **Post-hoc Rationalization:** If initial excuses prove insufficient, the brain upgrades and manufactures newer, better-sounding arguments—such as "If I didn't do it, someone else would have anyway."
* **Memory Distortion:** Over time, the brain distorts the memory of the event, remembering only angles where one was the victim or was forced to act, until genuinely convinced that the action was "right and justified."
